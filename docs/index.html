Who pays

If it hits you, someone owes you.

A 1972 UN treaty makes the country that launched an object absolutely liable for the damage it does on the ground. Every object on this site shows who that is.

100

countries have ratified the Liability Convention, as of 1 January 2025.

1

claim filed in 54 years. Canada, after Cosmos 954 scattered radioactive debris in 1978. The USSR paid C$3 million in 1981.

—

recorded finds can't be traced to any launching state, so there is no one to ask.

Who launched what was found

Article VIII

Your government claims.

You can't file yourself. The state where the damage happened, or whose citizen was hurt, presents the claim.

Article X

Within one year.

From the day of the damage, or from the day the launching state is identified.

Article XI

No court first.

The claim doesn't have to go through local courts before it's presented.

Article VII

Not your own rocket.

The treaty doesn't cover a country's own citizens. Debris from a US rocket in Texas falls under US law instead.

Launching states here come from CelesTrak's catalogue: the owner's country and the country of the launch site. The state that operated the rocket can be liable too, and when several qualify, any one can be asked for the full amount (Article V). This is a reading of public data, not legal advice.
